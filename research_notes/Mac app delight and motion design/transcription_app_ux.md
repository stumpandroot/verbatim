# UX patterns in transcription, dictation, voice-memo, and meeting-notes apps (Mac and iOS, 2024–2026)

Scope note: Apple's official pages for Voice Memos and Notes cover macOS 15 Sequoia, 26 Tahoe, and 27 but do not state which feature arrived in which version. Apple's "All New Features in macOS Tahoe" PDF and macwhisper.com could not be fetched in this session (permission withdrawn), so MacWhisper details come from 9to5Mac, MacSparky, and three third-party reviews. Two of the densest Wispr Flow and Granola sources are third-party "UX reference" notes hosted on jsDelivr (the `@agent-native/core` npm package); they are explicitly compiled from reviews and the authors' own research notes, so treat their pixel sizes and state descriptions as secondary, screenshot-derived observations rather than vendor specification.

## Key question 1: What does each app's home screen contain and how dense is it?

### Takeaway
The field splits into two home-screen models: (a) a chronological, date-grouped list of recordings with a search field and folders (Voice Memos, Granola, Wispr Flow's Hub, Superwhisper History), and (b) a document-first model where the transcript lives inside a note or page (Apple Notes, Notion). Reviewers consistently praise the flat, low-chroma list and criticize feature-heavy dashboards (Fireflies, tl;dv) as "overwhelming" or "cluttered."

### Cited Findings
- Apple Voice Memos (iOS 26): the main screen is a list of recordings above a bottom bar with a large red record button, a search button, and a back button; the back button leads to a home page that holds folders. Recordings are auto-named by location, which groups memos recorded in the same place. Favorites are set from a recording's three-dot menu and can be grouped in a folder. Folders can be reordered with a drag handle and renamed or deleted from a three-dot menu. — [iGeeksBlog, How to use Voice Memos in iOS 26](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-voice-memos-on-iphone-ipad/)
- Apple Voice Memos (Mac): search filters the list to recordings whose title *or transcription* contains the text; clicking the Transcription button then shows the matching transcript. — [Apple Support, View a transcription of a recording in Voice Memos on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/voice-memos/vm4a03609f0d)
- Apple Notes (Mac): there is no separate library. The recording sits in the note body alongside checklists, comments, and documents; double-clicking it opens an audio details section. Deleting the recording also deletes its transcript. — [Apple Support, Record and transcribe audio in Notes on Mac](https://support.apple.com/guide/notes/apdb5106e334)
- Granola: a left sidebar holds private and shared folders, team folders, a "shared with me" section, and a workspace switcher; the main pane is a chronological list grouped by day, with calendar events appearing automatically. Each row shows title, time, and attendees; past about two names, attendees collapse into a "+N" count. The style is "flat and low-chroma, closer to a notes app than a tile board." — [Granola UX Reference (third-party compiled notes)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/granola-ux.md)
- Granola co-founder Sam Stephenson: the home screen lists all past meetings; an LLM suggests a folder after a meeting but is not accurate enough to file automatically; an internal demo floated removing the sidebar entirely so "one interface [is] the only interface." — [Cognitive Revolution podcast with Sam Stephenson](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/calm-ai-for-crazy-days-inside-granola-s-design-philosophy-with-co-founder-sam-stephenson/)
- Granola: folders group notes by company or project, and Spaces separate personal from shared context; the reviewer praises the "purpose-built home" this gives meetings. — [Zack Proser, Granola vs Notion AI 2026](https://zackproser.com/blog/granola-vs-notion-ai-2026)
- Wispr Flow desktop ("the Hub"): Home shows transcript history grouped by date with search; hovering an entry reveals copy and flag actions; the page also shows word count, the push-to-talk shortcut (shown only until the user has dictated 2,000 words), and usage statistics. Up and Down arrow keys move through history entries. The sidebar lists Home, Meetings, Insights, Dictionary, Snippets, Style, Transforms, Notes, Tasks, Extensions, and Settings at the bottom; a collapsed sidebar shows labels on hover. — [Wispr Flow docs, Navigating the app](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android)
- Superwhisper History: each dictation is saved with audio, transcription, and AI result; a search field in the left sidebar filters past dictations (matching the original dictation only, not the AI result); an info icon opens a details sidebar; a reprocess icon below the recording re-runs it through a different mode. Retention is Forever by default with options from 1 day to 1 year. — [Superwhisper docs, History](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/history.md)
- Superwhisper criticism: audio recordings are "saved to disk by default with no opt-out toggle" (competitor-authored comparison, cites 23+ feedback votes). — [Spokenly blog comparison](https://spokenly.app/blog/wispr-flow-vs-superwhisper-vs-macwhisper)
- Aiko: shows the previous transcription at launch (can be turned off); its recorder keeps recent recordings for 7 days, accessible from the Files app; search was added in 1.3.0. No speaker detection, no in-app text editing. — [Aiko on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/app/id1672085276)
- Notion AI Meeting Notes: output is "a normal Notion page" with summary and transcript on the same page as the rest of the work; the reviewer calls this proximity Notion's "strongest advantage," but says it is "less appealing when the workspace is already cluttered" and adds decisions about "databases, permissions, and where each transcript belongs." — [Zack Proser, Granola vs Notion AI 2026](https://zackproser.com/blog/granola-vs-notion-ai-2026)
- Otter: "The dashboard is clean and intuitive. No learning curve." Fireflies: "Can be overwhelming. Lots of features, lots of menus... Not great for someone who just wants clean meeting notes." tl;dv: "Interface can feel cluttered. A lot going on for what should be a simple tool." — [Asian Efficiency, Best AI meeting note takers](https://www.asianefficiency.com/technology/best-ai-meeting-note-takers/)
- Otter: search by keyword, phrase, or speaker highlights results in the transcript; AI Chat works inside one transcript while Otter Chat queries the whole library (competitor-authored review). — [Sonix, 2025 Otter.ai review](https://sonix.ai/resources/?p=2637)
- MacWhisper: workflow starts with New Recording, Record App Audio, Global, Dictation, Files and Podcasts, and YouTube options; the interface is described as "straightforward" and the app "largely gets out of the way" after configuration. — [The Sweet Bits, MacWhisper review](https://thesweetbits.com/tools/macwhisper-review/)
- MacWhisper 14 added the ability to "favorite useful parts of a transcript." — [9to5Mac, MacWhisper 14 launches](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/14/macwhisper-14-launches-with-a-new-transcript-editor-faster-performance-more/)

### Inferences
- The praised home screens share three traits: date grouping as the default sort, one row per recording with minimal metadata (title, time, one secondary line), and a single search field that also matches transcript text. None of the praised apps lead with a card grid.
- Apps that store audio by default (Superwhisper) draw privacy complaints; Granola's decision to discard audio and keep only transcripts was framed by its co-founder as "far less creepy." A new app needs an explicit retention control at onboarding.
- Pinned items and favorites appear at the segment level (MacWhisper 14 favorites) and the recording level (Voice Memos favorites); no app surfaced "pinned" at the top of the list in the sources found.

### Gaps
- Exact row heights, preview text length, and whether apps show a transcript snippet in the list were not described in any text source; this would need screenshot inspection.
- Notion AI Meeting Notes and Otter home layouts were not described beyond general praise.
- Transcriptionist (Mac app) returned no usable sources; searches surfaced MacWhisper's App Store name "Whisper Transcription" instead.

## Key question 2: How is the record action presented and animated?

### Takeaway
Dictation apps converge on a global hotkey plus a small floating recorder (Wispr Flow's bottom-center dark pill, Superwhisper's resizable recording window with a mini mode, Granola's detachable green-bar pill), while recorder apps keep a big red button (Voice Memos) or an attachment-menu entry (Notes). The pill's fixed position is a recurring complaint.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Voice Memos (iOS 26): tapping the red record button starts recording immediately and shows a live waveform; progress appears in the Dynamic Island; swiping up the recorder tab reveals Pause, then Resume, Play to review, and a checkmark to save. Recording can continue in the background if no other audio plays. — [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-voice-memos-on-iphone-ipad/)
- Apple Voice Memos (Mac): click Record, click the Transcription button to see the transcript live; the waveform at the top of the window disappears temporarily and text appears with the current word highlighted; click Transcription again to return to the waveform; Pause/Resume; Done saves. — [Apple Support, Voice Memos](https://support.apple.com/guide/voice-memos/vm4a03609f0d)
- Apple Notes (Mac): click the Attachment toolbar button, then Record Audio; Record, Pause, Resume; Pause then Done to finish. You can keep editing the note while recording. To append, go to the end of a recording and press Record again. — [Apple Support, Notes](https://support.apple.com/guide/notes/apdb5106e334)
- Wispr Flow: a small floating bar at bottom-center of the screen; idle is a compact bubble that acts as entry point and status indicator (hideable in settings, and the source flags hiding as a common request); the canonical look is white animated bars on a dark capsule. Listening: the bar expands and shows moving white bars, and a start ping plays. Processing: on hotkey release the bars stop; there is no separate spinner. Done: the cleaned text is pasted at the cursor, which is the success signal. Esc cancels; audio under ~0.5 s is discarded. Push-to-talk is Fn on Mac; hands-free is Fn+Space (double-tap to start/stop). — [Wispr Flow UX Reference (third-party)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/wispr-ux.md)
- Wispr Flow official docs: the Flow Bar is the control used to dictate inside other apps; it can be shown or hidden in System settings and after setup can be dragged to the left, right, or bottom edge. Menu bar icon offers Open Wispr Flow, Help center, Talk to support. — [Wispr Flow docs](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android)
- Wispr Flow pill complaint: the pill is "locked to bottom-center"; PillFloat is an open-source menu bar utility that uses Accessibility APIs to move it anywhere so users can "set your position, forget it exists." — [Feedbagel, PillFloat](https://feedbagel.com/post/pillfloat-open-source-tool-to-reposition-wispr-flows-dictation-pill-on-macos)
- Wispr Flow design praise: "Wispr Flow appears as a dark, floating panel over whatever app you're using"; "The UI asks almost nothing of the user"; a subtle waveform animation plays while listening; praised for restraint and no mode changes or separate editing stage. — [Abduzeedo](https://abduzeedo.com/node/89253)
- Superwhisper: system-wide hotkey ("Hit Option-Space anywhere on macOS, speak, and the text appears"); its recording window overlay is called "intrusive for some users" (competitor-authored). — [Spokenly comparison](https://spokenly.app/blog/wispr-flow-vs-superwhisper-vs-macwhisper)
- Superwhisper recording window: controls are a resize toggle (main vs mini view), a live waveform, a mode display (click to switch, or Option+Shift+K; in Super Mode it shows the current app or website context), Stop, and Cancel (Esc; recordings under 30 s cancel immediately, longer ones ask to confirm). A context indicator lights up when clipboard or selected text is captured. With a realtime-capable model the window shows words as you speak. The mini window keeps core controls in compact form, can stay visible as a small indicator, shows the waveform while recording, reveals a stop button on hover, and right-click offers Expand Window, Open Settings, Open History. — [Superwhisper docs, Recording window](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-rec-window.md)
- Superwhisper menu bar: "Start Recording on Menubar Click" makes left-click start/stop and right-click open the menu; "Always close" closes the recording window after each dictation; "Show in Dock" toggle. — [Superwhisper docs, Advanced settings](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/settings-advanced)
- Granola: the recording pill sits bottom-center of the note view above the chat input; clicking it opens the live transcript. When you switch apps it detaches and floats on the right edge with a drag handle; clicking returns you to the note. It shows animated vertical green bars while capturing, which freeze when paused; it hides when idle and stops automatically when the call seems to end, after ~15 minutes of silence, or on system sleep. Estimated collapsed size ~120–160 × 32–40 px, expanding to a 360–420 px panel. A macOS notification at meeting time offers a single "Start Granola" action. No documented shortcuts for start/stop. — [Granola UX Reference (third-party)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/granola-ux.md)
- Granola captures at the OS audio level rather than joining as a bot; Stephenson contrasts this with "this huge glowing orb with a logo on it," and says real-time transcription that shows text as you speak is "very comforting." — [Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/calm-ai-for-crazy-days-inside-granola-s-design-philosophy-with-co-founder-sam-stephenson/)
- Bots as a UX negative: "'Otter.ai has joined the meeting.' Some clients get uncomfortable"; Fireflies likewise. — [Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/technology/best-ai-meeting-note-takers/)
- MacWhisper: drop a file into the window and transcription starts "with no confirmation step or setup screen"; dictation uses the Globe/Fn key as push-to-talk (hold, speak, release); from the home screen you can capture audio from an open app and transcribe it in real time. — [Dave Swift, MacWhisper review](https://daveswift.com/macwhisper/)
- MacWhisper: hold a hotkey to dictate into most text fields "without opening a separate window"; Global mode launches from anywhere and sends text to the clipboard or another app; the Dictation experience is "remarkably frictionless." — [The Sweet Bits](https://thesweetbits.com/tools/macwhisper-review/)
- MacWhisper auto-detects meetings in Zoom, Teams, Webex, Skype, Chime, and Discord (competitor-authored review). — [Voibe, MacWhisper review](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/macwhisper-review/)
- Aiko: no live transcription while recording; the iOS app must stay open during transcription. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/app/id1672085276)

### Inferences
- The dominant "delightful" dictation pattern is: hotkey → small dark capsule with live bars → bars freeze on release → text appears. The absence of a spinner is deliberate; the paste itself is the completion state.
- Fixed-position pills generate friction; a draggable pill (Granola, Wispr Flow after setup) or an edge-snapping option avoids the PillFloat-style workaround.
- Green animated bars (Granola) and white bars on dark (Wispr Flow) are the two visual signatures; a new app should pick a single accent for "live audio" and reuse it in the list row for an in-progress recording.

### Gaps
- No source gives animation timings, easing, or the exact transition between idle bubble and expanded bar for any app.
- The macOS 26 Voice Memos window chrome (Liquid Glass toolbar, record button placement on Mac) was not described in text sources; only the iOS 26 layout was.

## Key question 3: How do the best transcript views handle speaker labels, corrections, and playback sync?

### Takeaway
Apple sets the baseline for playback sync (current word highlighted during live transcription; click any line or term to seek). MacWhisper 14 and Descript set the baseline for correction (edit in place, reassign speakers, find-and-replace, keyboard-driven correction), and Granola's "Me"/"Them" chat-bubble transcript is the simplest speaker model but reviewers want real names.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Voice Memos (Mac): during live transcription the current word is highlighted; after recording, selecting a term in the transcription moves the playhead to that point in the waveform; transcript text can be selected and copied via Control-click; with Apple Intelligence, Writing Tools can summarize, proofread, and adjust tone. — [Apple Support, Voice Memos](https://support.apple.com/guide/voice-memos/vm4a03609f0d)
- Apple Voice Memos (iOS 26): tap the transcript icon in the left corner to see a live transcript "with words highlighted as audio plays"; transcripts are searchable; three-dot menu > Copy Transcript. — [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-voice-memos-on-iphone-ipad/)
- Apple Notes (Mac): double-click the recording, click Transcription; click any line of the transcript to play from that point; More menu offers Find in Transcript, Copy Transcript, Add Transcript to Note; with Apple Intelligence a Summary button appears. Live transcription needs M1 or later. — [Apple Support, Notes](https://support.apple.com/guide/notes/apdb5106e334)
- MacWhisper 14 "Editor View": "edit your transcript directly in the main transcript screen," easier paragraph insertion, "reassign speakers faster," favorite useful parts, improved Cmd+A, "instant editor interaction," better playback with several transcripts open, and a fix for audio/video scrub syncing. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/14/macwhisper-14-launches-with-a-new-transcript-editor-faster-performance-more/); corroborated by [MacSparky](https://www.macsparky.com/blog/2026/07/macwhisper-14-adds-a-new-editor-view/)
- MacWhisper editing: change segments, ignore sections, reassign speakers, adjust timestamps, find-and-replace for frequent errors, font size and transcript view switching; an integrated video player keeps transcript and synchronized subtitles beside the video; speakers are identified automatically and renamed afterward; the live meeting view shows speakers as they talk, lets you rename or merge them during recording, and can float above other windows; diarization supports up to eight speakers and "still needs manual correction." — [The Sweet Bits](https://thesweetbits.com/tools/macwhisper-review/)
- MacWhisper diarization "is in beta and can split one speaker into several." — [Voibe](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/macwhisper-review/); same complaint in [Dave Swift](https://daveswift.com/macwhisper/)
- MacWhisper supports "speaker photos" (competitor-authored). — [Spokenly comparison](https://spokenly.app/blog/wispr-flow-vs-superwhisper-vs-macwhisper)
- Descript correction: highlight wrong text and click Correct at the bottom of the screen, or press `e`; pressing `e` pauses playback and resumes after the edit; hold `w` on a word to cycle period/comma/none with automatic recapitalization; hold `q` to toggle case; Cmd+R inserts a speaker break that defaults to a different speaker; drag a speaker label to move the boundary; "Speaker Detective" adds labels automatically; a transcription glossary improves future recognition. — [Tuts+, How to correct AI transcriptions in Descript](https://photography.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-correct-ai-transcriptions-descript--cms-36948)
- Granola transcript panel: chat-bubble layout, grey bubbles left for system audio ("Them"), green bubbles right for microphone ("Me"); desktop labels only Me and Them; copy, chunk management, and in-transcript search; timestamps sit inline with bubbles rather than in a separate column; summary bullets link to the matching transcript moment via a magnifying-glass icon; recommended improvement: show real speaker names. — [Granola UX Reference (third-party)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/granola-ux.md)
- Granola note view: user notes in black on the left, AI summary in gray beside or below; edits to AI text turn black; title edited inline with no edit button; the AI pane stays empty during the meeting and fills only after "Enhance notes" (auto at call end or manual, re-runnable with another template); loading is quiet with no modal or heavy spinner; errors show a simple retry. — same source.
- Granola does not save audio for playback and cannot import existing MP3s. — [Zack Proser](https://zackproser.com/blog/granola-vs-notion-ai-2026)
- Notion AI Meeting Notes: transcript citations link summary claims back to the conversation. — [Zack Proser](https://zackproser.com/blog/granola-vs-notion-ai-2026)
- Otter: text appears within seconds; each speaker gets a unique label but detection was inconsistent (labeled one speaker, then three after reload, in a two-person interview); sentence breaks "often required significant manual editing"; shared transcripts can be edited and commented on. — [Sonix review of Otter (competitor)](https://sonix.ai/resources/?p=2637)
- Wispr Flow: no streaming partials; text appears only on release as one paste; "View Diff" (Fn+D) shows raw vs cleaned text; Scratchpad (Opt+S) lets users dictate into a floating window and edit before pasting. — [Wispr Flow UX Reference (third-party)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/wispr-ux.md)
- Aiko: exports as subtitles and text with timestamps (timestamp display is a setting); no speaker detection; no in-app editing ("export and edit elsewhere"). — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/app/id1672085276)

### Inferences
- The strongest transcript views combine three things no single app fully does: Apple-grade word-level highlight and click-to-seek, MacWhisper-grade in-place editing and speaker reassignment, and Descript-grade keyboard correction (pause-on-edit, resume-after). A third-party app that does all three would exceed the field.
- Speaker diarization is the most-criticized feature across MacWhisper and Otter; the UI should assume errors and make merge/rename/reassign one-click, as MacWhisper's live meeting view does.
- Granola's black-vs-gray distinction for human vs AI text is a low-cost, widely praised pattern for showing provenance without chips or badges.

### Gaps
- No source described speaker "chips" visually (color, avatar, placement) for any app except Granola's bubbles and MacWhisper's speaker photos (mentioned, not described).
- Whether Voice Memos on macOS 26 allows editing the transcript text was not stated; Apple's page describes only copy and Writing Tools.

## Key question 4: Which apps use illustration, color, or character, and how is it received?

### Takeaway
The praised apps use almost no illustration or character. Wispr Flow is dark and "understated," Granola is "calm, paper-like, and low-chroma," and Apple uses a red record button, yellow trim handles, and a live waveform. Restraint is explicitly cited as the reason these feel good; feature-dense competitors are the ones criticized.

### Cited Findings
- Wispr Flow: base palette is dark; the personal dictionary panel is the exception, "using a light-mode list that contrasts with the otherwise dark product look"; styling is "intentionally understated"; the homepage sells speed with a side-by-side motion graphic (keyboard ~45 wpm vs voice ~220 wpm) rather than copy. — [Abduzeedo](https://abduzeedo.com/node/89253)
- Granola: black text for user content, gray for AI content; green accent for live bars and "Me" bubbles; "calm, paper-like, and low-chroma"; dark mode exists; sans-serif typography refreshed February 2026; density medium-low with generous padding. — [Granola UX Reference (third-party)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/granola-ux.md)
- Granola's stated design goal: "a calm product experience for people with crazy workdays," inspired by Oxo's design-for-extreme-users approach; the team is "almost paranoid" about adding anything to the core meeting view, which must "pass a really high bar"; templates are deliberately hidden ("it's way too hidden") for power users; chat moved from a sidebar to a floating element after a one-day prototype. — [Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/calm-ai-for-crazy-days-inside-granola-s-design-philosophy-with-co-founder-sam-stephenson/)
- Granola delight anecdotes: notes "popped up in Slack" 30 seconds after a meeting; users react emotionally to the "Coach Me Matt" recipe; users "feel more present in the meeting." — same source.
- Apple Voice Memos (iOS 26): yellow trim handles on the waveform, pinch to zoom the waveform, turtle/rabbit icons on the playback-speed slider, Enhance Recording toggle, Skip Silence toggle. — [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-voice-memos-on-iphone-ipad/)
- macOS Tahoe 26 Liquid Glass: sidebars in Safari, Music, Podcasts, and News and toolbars in Mail, Notes, and Messages are designed "to keep attention on content"; the menu bar is transparent; windows have a rounder corner radius; buttons, sliders, and switches "respond with Liquid Glass animations during interaction." — [iClarified, Every new feature in macOS Tahoe 26](https://www.iclarified.com/98494/every-new-feature-in-macos-tahoe-26-list)
- Aiko supports Reduced Motion, Differentiate Without Color, and Sufficient Contrast accessibility settings. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/app/id1672085276)
- Feature-dense tools are the ones criticized: Fireflies "overwhelming," tl;dv "cluttered." — [Asian Efficiency](https://www.asianefficiency.com/technology/best-ai-meeting-note-takers/)
- Superwhisper onboarding: "new users report feeling overwhelmed" by the power-user setup; Wispr Flow has "more polished onboarding" (competitor-authored). — [Spokenly comparison](https://spokenly.app/blog/wispr-flow-vs-superwhisper-vs-macwhisper)

### Inferences
- "Character" in this category comes from motion (live bars, word highlight, speed animation) and from a single accent color, not from mascots or illustration. No source found praises an illustrated or mascot-driven transcription app.
- The one place a light panel inside a dark product was noted (Wispr Flow's dictionary) reads as a deliberate "this is your data, not the machine's" cue; it parallels Granola's black-vs-gray provenance.

### Gaps
- No source discusses app icons, empty-state illustrations, or onboarding artwork for any of these apps.
- No hex values or type specimens are published for Granola or Wispr Flow; the third-party reference explicitly says exact values are not published.

## Key question 5: What do macOS 26 Voice Memos and Notes do that a third-party app should match?

### Takeaway
Apple's bar is: live transcript toggle that swaps the waveform for text with the current word highlighted, click-to-seek from any term or line, transcript search from the library, copy/add-to-note/summary actions, layered recording with a Layer Mix slider (record on iPhone 16 Pro, play/edit on Mac 15.2+), and recording embedded in a document (Notes) while you keep typing.

### Cited Findings
- Voice Memos (Mac, macOS 15–27 guide): Transcription button toggles between waveform and transcript during and after recording; current word highlighted live; selecting a term seeks; search matches titles and transcriptions; recordings from macOS 14 or earlier are transcribed automatically when opened; Writing Tools summarize/proofread/rewrite with Apple Intelligence. — [Apple Support, Voice Memos](https://support.apple.com/guide/voice-memos/vm4a03609f0d)
- Voice Memos (iOS 26): big red record button, live waveform, Dynamic Island status, swipe-up pause/resume, auto-naming by location, folders and favorites, Trim with yellow handles and pinch-zoom, Replace (overwrite a section from the playhead), Enhance Recording, Playback Speed slider, Skip Silence, Mono/Stereo/Spatial recording modes and Lossy/Lossless quality, Recently Deleted (30 days), iCloud sync, share as .m4a (loses Spatial and layer effects) or Options > Editable > Save to Files to keep them. Transcripts require iPhone 12 or later. — [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-voice-memos-on-iphone-ipad/)
- Layered recording: requires iPhone 16 Pro/Pro Max on iOS 18.2+; iPads (18.2) and Macs (macOS Sequoia 15.2+) can play and mix layered memos but not record layers. Flow: select recording → waveform icon → "+" → red record → stop; the new layer stacks over the original. Mixing: waveform button → three-line icon → Playback Speed slider, Skip Silence, enhancement toggle, Layer Mix slider, X to close. "Separate Layers" from the three-dot menu saves each layer as its own recording. — [iGeeksBlog, Layered Voice Memos](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-layered-voice-memos-on-iphone)
- Notes (Mac): record from the Attachment menu, keep editing the note while recording, rename inline by clicking the bold title, append by pressing Record at the end, click a line to seek, Find in Transcript, Copy Transcript, Add Transcript to Note, Summary (Apple Intelligence), share/save/delete from Control-click. Requires M1+ and iCloud notes; supported transcription languages listed (English regions, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, German, Japanese, Korean, Simplified and Traditional Chinese). — [Apple Support, Notes](https://support.apple.com/guide/notes/apdb5106e334)
- macOS Tahoe 26 Notes can capture a FaceTime audio or phone call, saving a transcript and the audio, plus a summary; Phone shows live transcription on speaker calls; Apple Intelligence voicemail summaries appear next to missed calls. — [iClarified](https://www.iclarified.com/98494/every-new-feature-in-macos-tahoe-26-list)
- Voice Memos integration: Notes integration lets you record a Voice Memo inside a note. — [iGeeksBlog](https://www.igeeksblog.com/how-to-use-voice-memos-on-iphone-ipad/)

### Inferences
- A third-party Mac app competing with Voice Memos needs, at minimum: a waveform-to-transcript toggle (not a separate pane), word-level highlight, click-to-seek, transcript-aware library search, and copy/summary actions. Layered recording is an iPhone 16 Pro hardware feature and not a reasonable parity target on Mac.
- Apple's models leave gaps a third-party app can own: speaker labels (absent from Voice Memos and Notes per the support pages), editing the transcript text, exports beyond copy, and local-model choice.
- Because Apple requires iCloud notes and limits languages by region, "works offline, any language, no account" (Aiko, MacWhisper) remains a differentiator.

### Gaps

Treat any claim about a Liquid Glass Voice Memos window as unverified.
- No source described the Mac Voice Memos window layout (sidebar, list density, record button placement).

## Supplementary: processing states, exports, dictionary features, and onboarding

### Takeaway
Dictionary features split into pre-recognition hints (Superwhisper Vocabulary, Descript glossary, Wispr Flow dictionary that auto-learns from edits) and post-recognition rules (Superwhisper Replacements, Aiko word replacement, MacWhisper find-and-replace). Onboarding is praised when it is a short permission-card sequence with a mic test and a sandbox (Wispr Flow) and criticized when it exposes power-user setup first (Superwhisper).

### Cited Findings
- Superwhisper Vocabulary: words are sent with the audio as recognition hints; also influence punctuation and formatting; keep the list short because too many words confuse the model. Replacements run after transcription "exactly as defined, without AI interpretation"; matching ignores capitalization (one entry catches todo/ToDo/TODO) and output uses the capitalization you set; common uses are spoken symbols ("at sign" → @, "dot com" → .com) and email addresses. Both share one interface with a trash icon per entry, plus CLI commands `superwhisper vocab` and `superwhisper snippets`. — [Superwhisper docs, Vocabulary](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-vocabulary.md)
- Wispr Flow: Dictionary auto-learns corrections from edits with manual entry for names and jargon; Snippets are voice-triggered text expansion; Style has tone presets and four cleanup intensity levels; Team plan shares dictionary and snippets. Onboarding: browser sign-in → permission cards for Microphone and Accessibility → mic test with the waveform and input device picker → hotkey setup (Fn default) → profile/privacy/language → "Try It Yourself" sandbox with simulated Gmail and Notion → Transform before/after demo → Flow Hub. — [Wispr Flow UX Reference (third-party)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/wispr-ux.md)
- Wispr Flow Data & Privacy settings: storage normal, auto-delete after 24 hours, or disabled; Cloud Sync; data export; Reset app deletes local data and re-syncs dictionary and settings. — [Wispr Flow docs](https://docs.wisprflow.ai/articles/5096240724-navigating-the-wispr-flow-app-desktop-ios-and-android)
- Wispr Flow criticism: cloud-only with no offline mode; ~800 MB RAM idle on Windows; Privacy Mode opt-in; Trustpilot reliability complaints after the trial (competitor-authored). — [Spokenly comparison](https://spokenly.app/blog/wispr-flow-vs-superwhisper-vs-macwhisper)
- Descript glossary: add uncommon words to improve future transcriptions. — [Tuts+](https://photography.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-correct-ai-transcriptions-descript--cms-36948)
- Aiko: word-replacement feature for automatically correcting common mistakes; option to remove silent parts (1.10.0); ~1.8 GB download because Whisper large-v3 ships on Mac; languages cannot be removed to save space. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ch/app/app/id1672085276)
- MacWhisper model choice on first launch is framed as a speed/accuracy/language/memory trade-off; exports TXT, Markdown, PDF, HTML, DOCX, SRT, VTT. — [The Sweet Bits](https://thesweetbits.com/tools/macwhisper-review/); Voibe adds CSV. — [Voibe](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/macwhisper-review/)
- MacWhisper exports use the standard Mac save dialog; a batch exporter outputs several formats at once; watch folders auto-transcribe and export. — [Dave Swift](https://daveswift.com/macwhisper/)
- MacWhisper 14 redesigned the AI services screen to be "clearer and easier to use"; 14.1 added Gladia; cloud uploads no longer load whole files into memory. — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/07/14/macwhisper-14-launches-with-a-new-transcript-editor-faster-performance-more/)
- MacWhisper buyer confusion: two products share the name (Gumroad "MacWhisper" vs App Store "Whisper Transcription"), with different pricing; the App Store version lacks system-wide dictation; many features are Pro-only. — [Voibe](https://www.getvoibe.com/resources/macwhisper-review/); Pro gating also in [The Sweet Bits](https://thesweetbits.com/tools/macwhisper-review/)
- Superwhisper system-audio capture: the waveform stays still by design while recording continues; users verify in History. Voice model active duration setting trades memory for faster next dictation. — [Superwhisper docs, Recording window](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/interface-rec-window.md); [Advanced settings](https://superwhisper.com/docs/get-started/settings-advanced)
- Granola onboarding: install, sign in, grant calendar, grant mic and screen-recording permissions, land on the populated meeting list; no tour. Sharing is criticized for a multi-step flow. — [Granola UX Reference (third-party)](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/@agent-native/core@0.124.6/corpus/templates/clips/desktop/design-refs/granola-ux.md)
- Apple's own progress state: recordings from older macOS are "transcribed automatically when opened" (no visible queue described). — [Apple Support, Voice Memos](https://support.apple.com/guide/voice-memos/vm4a03609f0d)

### Inferences
- The clearest dictionary UX has two lists with different mental models: "help it hear" (hints) and "fix what it wrote" (literal replacements). Superwhisper's single shared interface with a per-row trash icon is the simplest shipping version; Wispr Flow's auto-learn from edits is the lowest-friction.
- No source praised any model-download or transcribing progress screen; the delight stories are about absence of waiting (Granola notes in Slack in 30 seconds, Wispr paste "almost instantly"). A progress state should be quiet, inline, and never modal.
- A system-audio recording that shows a flat waveform (Superwhisper) is confusing enough that the vendor documents it; a new app should show a distinct "capturing system audio" indicator instead.

### Gaps
- No source describes MacWhisper's model-download progress UI, its custom vocabulary (if any), or its onboarding permission sequence.
- Notion AI Meeting Notes, Otter, and Descript onboarding and permission flows were not covered by any fetched source.
- Transcriptionist and the 2025–2026 newcomers (VoiceInk, Monologue, Typeless, Spokenly, Handy, Voibe) surfaced only in competitor comparison pages; no reviewer descriptions of their UI were obtained within the tool budget.
