# Verbatim: brand positioning and messaging guide

**Working draft for review | October 7, 2026**

This guide applies selected principles from Stump & Root's Position First, So You Can Messaging, StoryBrand / Marketing Made Simple, 3 Vs, Golden Circle, Client Intake, and Design and Presentation Guide to Verbatim. The frameworks are references for how to work, not evidence that this product's audience and positioning have been validated. Distinguish proposed messages from proven facts. Preserve the approved visual identity and application behavior.

## 1. The actual customer problem

**What happens today:** useful statements are buried in online videos. Someone watches, forgets a quote, rewinds repeatedly, or tries to take notes while listening. Even when captions are available, they can be hard to find and reuse. Automated summaries sometimes miss context or introduce errors.

**Desired result:** find what was said, retrieve a specific point, and keep notes without repeatedly watching the video. Verify important details against the original.

**Working first audience (NOT yet validated):** people who routinely use videos for learning or work and value capturing source material. This may include students, researchers, creators, marketers, and working professionals; choose a primary segment before committing to a category-specific campaign. Do not imply the product has already been validated by any of these groups.

## 2. Position First: Promise / Proof / POV / Packaging

**Working promise:** Go from an accessible video link to a transcript you can search, check, and use.

**Product-supported proof points:** caption-first extraction (when accessible); transcription fallback for accessible audio; timestamped transcript; original text preserved separately from AI editing; optional cleaned transcript, key points, and review flags; TXT/SRT/VTT exports; support paths for 15 platform URLs. Note: live compatibility and accuracy are not proven for every video or service. These are capabilities/implementation facts, not customer-testimonials or guarantees.

**Point of view:** Important ideas should be easier to return to. AI may organize words, but it should never silently replace the source.

**Packaging:** A fast entry point (paste link), a source-first transcript workspace, and optional AI notes. Three main sections on iPhone: Home, Library, Settings. One primary call to action at a time.

## 3. StoryBrand: user's story

- **Customer/hero:** wants to find and retain important information in a video.
- **External problem:** source material is locked inside long or scattered videos.
- **Internal problem (hypothesis):** it feels inefficient to constantly pause, rewind, and manually capture what matters.
- **Philosophical problem (hypothesis):** useful ideas should not vanish because they were spoken rather than written.
- **Guide:** Verbatim offers practical tools rather than presenting itself as the hero.
- **Plan:** Paste a link → Get a searchable transcript → Clean it up or summarize if needed.
- **Direct CTA:** Get transcript.
- **Transitional CTA:** Try a sample.
- **Successful outcome:** the information is readable, searchable, checkable, and reusable.
- **Avoid:** claiming perfect accuracy, automatic access to every site, instant results, or completely trustworthy AI output.

## 4. 3 Vs (draft only)

- **Villain (the obstacle, not people or platforms):** useful information buried in video playback and unreliable, uncheckable text.
- **Victim/ICP:** people whose work or learning depends on material shared in video.
- **Vow:** keep the original words accessible and give users a clearer way to find and use them.

Avoid overaggressive enemy language and do not claim other transcription products are inherently unreliable without substantiation.

## 5. Golden Circle (provisional)

**WHY:** We believe useful ideas deserve to stay useful after a video ends.

**HOW:** Search for existing captions first; transcribe accessible audio if needed; keep the source separate from AI revisions; surface uncertain wording for human review.

**WHAT:** Searchable transcripts, cleaned versions, summaries, review notes, and download formats for accessible online videos.

## 6. Brand personality and voice

**Clear, useful, warm, lightly playful, and credible.**

- Lead with specific outcomes, then explain the feature.
- Use active, everyday words: paste, find, search, check, save, copy, download.
- One sentence should answer the next user question; don't make them infer what a button does.
- Let illustration and motion provide personality. Use straightforward UI copy in tasks, tabs, and errors.
- Call out uncertainty and limits plainly; an unverified AI inference is not an original quote.
- No emoji or generic artificial-intelligence sparkle language.
- Avoid empty superlatives, repeated clever headlines, guilt-inducing language, and false certainty.
- Keep capitalization conventional; use concise sentence case for UI. Maintain approved wordmark case only where appropriate.

**Voice by state:**

- **Home:** approachable and concise: “Turn videos into searchable text.”
- **Processing:** calm and factual: “Checking for existing captions.”
- **Transcript:** stable and precise: “Original transcript”; “Search transcript.”
- **AI notes:** transparent: “AI-edited text may contain errors. Compare important phrases with the original.”
- **Success:** low-friction: “Transcript ready.”
- **Error:** actionable: “We couldn’t access captions or audio for this video. Try another link.”

## 8. Vocabulary / consistency

| Use | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Original transcript** | The initial caption or speech-recognition output, which can contain errors |
| **Cleaned transcript** | AI-edited version; not verified against audio by default |
| **Summary / key points** | AI-generated interpretation of the transcript, not a verbatim source |
| **Review flags** | Suggested phrases to confirm; not proof of mistakes |
| **Captions** | Existing caption/subtitle text from source/platform |
| **Transcription** | Audio converted to text when captions aren't accessible |
| **Supported link** | Accepted source-platform URL; not a guarantee that media is accessible |
| **Saved library** | Stored locally in the browser; not cross-device sync |

Do not use “accurate,” “perfect,” “every word,” “instant,” “any video,” or “verified quote” without evidence appropriate to that claim.

## 9. Screen-specific principles

- **Home:** single job: paste link and start. Fits initial iPhone viewport, no unnecessary feature explainer on compact phones. Keep platform marks and balanced 3-item bottom navigation.
- **Processing:** show real processing stage. Do not present decorative stage-percent as a guaranteed ETA. Say when the link cannot be accessed.
- **Result:** Original first; timestamp and source type visible. Give an explicit path to clean text, summary, review flags, copy, and export. Keep the user in control.
- **Library:** say what is stored and where. No false suggestion of cloud sync.
- **Settings:** explain local processing / required external AI service / differences from native iPhone app.

## 10. Important open questions before treating this as final

1. Which user comes first: students, working professionals, content creators, or researchers? Their language and proof needs differ.
2. Is the biggest outcome remembering key points, making clips/captions, extracting research, or saving time? Test a small set of promises rather than picking by preference alone.
3. What can be validated in real use: platform compatibility by source, typical processing time, transcription quality, and error correction rate?
4. Where will transcripts be stored long term? Browser-local persistence is current functionality, not a reliable cross-device archive.
5. Should the app support private/logged-in videos or only openly accessible URLs? State the limit clearly in onboarding.
6. Do users trust AI-cleaned wording? Test source-comparison and review flag usability.
7. Confirm whether “Real words. More possibilities.” is an approved consumer tagline or a concept line. It is not strong enough as the only explanation of the product.
