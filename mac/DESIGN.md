# Verbatim design rules

This file is the brand spec for every Verbatim surface: the Mac app, the iPhone app, the keyboard, the dictation bar, the menu bar, widgets and Live Activities, the app icon, and the website. Agents read it before changing any UI. If something you need isn't here, ask; don't invent a color, a typeface, an icon, a material, or a radius.

Sources: the family design system (project doc `01-design-system.md`, v2.1), the brand marks decision (`brand/README.md`, October 18, 2026), the interface icon set (October 18, 2026), Tim's Home review (October 22, 2026), his Home feel choices the same day (sharp corners, stacked tiles, full motion), and the refined look ("Verbose Refined Look", October 28, 2026; §13). `mac/tools/brand-check.sh` enforces the rules marked **[checked]**.

## 1. Color

The palette lives in `mac/Verbatim/Views/Tokens.swift` (`Refined`, built from the `RefinedHex` table; every value is in §13's Tokens). Use tokens only. **[checked]** No hex values outside `Tokens.swift`, and no `Color.red`, `.blue`, `.orange`, `.green`, `.gray`, `.purple`, `.pink`, `.yellow`, or `.accentColor` outside it. Errors and destructive actions use `Refined.danger` (defined in Tokens.swift), with an icon and a word, never persimmon.

| Token | Hex (light) | Means | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `paper` | `#F5F0E6` | The page | App background |
| `reader` / `card` | `#FFFDF9` | Content you read | Transcript sheet, neutral cards, rows |
| `ink` | `#26352E` | Structure | Text, strokes, strong actions |
| `persimmon` | `#FB7350` | **Record** | Record panel, voice-note chips, recording state, today's bar in This week, the app icon |
| `butter` | `#F4C76B` | **Dictate** | Dictate tile |
| `sage` | `#AFC398` | **Import** (files and links) | Import tile, imported-file and link chips |
| `petal` | `#EFB6C8` | **App audio** | App audio tile, app-audio chips |
| `card` | — | **Live captions** | Live captions tile and its chips |

Neutral (`ink` at 6% with `dim` text, plus the item's own diagram or initials): kinds (journal, thoughts, meeting, note, podcast, video, talk, interview, brain dump, audio only), plans (§17.7), tags, projects, filters, counts, the transcript's unclear chips (§15), and the icon tiles on settings rows (the wash with an ink glyph; destructive rows keep the wash with the `danger` glyph and word). They never borrow an action hue.

Speaker pills in a transcript are the one exception: they may use the four fills (`persimmon`, `petal`, `sage`, `butter`) in a fixed rotation by speaker order (first speaker record, then petal, sage, butter, and around again), with ink text and the speaker's name. The name carries the meaning; the hue only tells speakers apart. Everywhere else, color still means a capture mode.

Reading washes are a separate role and appear only on the transcript and document surfaces, never beside the action tiles: the line being played takes the `playing` wash (sage), and its time `sageText`. Playback follows the playing color: the played part of the waveform is `played`. Moments and bookmarks are accent marks, never a wash (§6.9).

Rules:

1. One color, one meaning. Never reuse a hue for a different action on the same screen. Tags, filters, and counts are neutral (filter chips use the `neutral` wash, §13). Two exceptions: project chips, which take a hue by the project's name (`RefinedRules.projectHue`; their text says the project's name, so the hue never stands alone), and speaker pills in a transcript, which rotate record, petal, sage, and butter (above; the name is always on the pill).
2. About 70% neutral, 20% ink, 10% accent on working screens. Home and onboarding may run richer.
3. One saturated fill per screen: persimmon, for the primary action. The pastel start tiles on Home (butter, sage, petal) are allowed because each one is a different action; everything else is a tint or neutral.
4. Text on paper uses `ink`, `dim`, `mute`, or a text-safe variant (`accentText`, `sageText`), never a bright fill.
5. Text on every fill is ink: petal 7.5:1, butter 8.1:1, sage 6.8:1, persimmon 4.45:1 (so on persimmon, ink text must be 18 pt or larger, or semibold 14 pt or larger). Never put reader white text on persimmon; it measures 2.8:1.
6. Status never depends on color alone; pair it with a word or an icon.
7. Dark mode is a near-black green, never an inversion. The fills get slightly deeper values in dark only (`persimmon` `#F27A57`, `butter` `#DDB563`, `sage` `#9DB088`, `petal` `#D9A3B5`). Ink text (`onFill`) stays on them in both modes.

## 2. Type

| Role | Font | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Display | `Refined.serif` (New York stands in for Instrument Serif) | Greeting, Record, section titles, document titles, big numbers |
| UI and body | SF Pro, Dynamic Type | Everything else: tile names, labels, rows, buttons, transcript text |
| Data | `Refined.mono` (SF Pro with tabular digits) | Keyboard shortcuts, timecodes and durations, running recording timers, and counts. Never smaller than 12 pt. |

Rules:

1. Sentence case everywhere. No uppercase tracked labels.
2. Metadata lines are sans, not mono: "Voice note · 7:50 · Today". Use `.monospacedDigit()` for numbers that change. Data text is never smaller than 12 pt (`mac/tools/test-live-activity.sh` checks the widgets and the keyboard).
3. Shortcuts render as keycaps: mono inside an outlined rectangle with the button radius (4).
4. Never set reading text in the serif, except on screens built on the refined kit, where transcript reading text is serif (§13).
5. Button names: SF Pro semibold (13.5 pt in a regular pill, 12 pt in a mini). Mac Home's tile names are serif 21 pt with a 12 pt sans detail (§6.5).

## 3. Icons

- **Ours (`BrandArt`) for anything that means something in Verbatim:** record, dictate, import, link, app audio, captions, library, moments, bookmark, play, export, summaries, speakers, language, voice, search inside a transcript, empty states, and all navigation. Fine line: a 24-unit grid with a 1.5 stroke, or a 112-unit box with a 6 stroke. Round caps and joins.
- **Apple's SF Symbols only for standard controls.** **[checked]** Allowed: `xmark`, `xmark.circle.fill`, `plus`, `plus.circle`, `minus.circle`, `chevron.*`, `checkmark`, `checkmark.circle.fill`, `ellipsis`, `ellipsis.circle`, `square.and.arrow.up`, `trash`, `magnifyingglass` (search fields only), `sidebar.left`, `line.3.horizontal` (drag handles), `arrow.right` (text links), `arrow.up.arrow.down`, `chevron.up.chevron.down`, `arrow.uturn.backward`, `arrow.counterclockwise`, and keyboard keys (`return`, `delete.left`, `globe`, `keyboard`, `shift`, `shift.fill`, `capslock.fill`).
- Everything else, such as `sparkles`, `wand.and.stars`, `waveform`, `mic`, `bookmark`, `link`, `play.*`, `note.text`, `doc.on.clipboard`, and `info.circle`, gets a `BrandArt` glyph. If none exists, draw one in the same grammar and add it to `mac/tools/gen_icons.py`.
- **System surfaces** (Control Center controls, Live Activities and the Dynamic Island, widgets, context menus, the menu bar item) can only show SF Symbols or custom symbols from the asset catalog. Export our glyphs as custom symbols (`.symbols` in Assets) and use those; until a glyph is exported, the closest SF Symbol is allowed there, marked on its line with `// brand: system surface`. The menu bar item uses the bars mark. Exported (`mac/tools/gen_symbol.py`): `thought`, `mark`, `marked`, `record` (capture), `dictate` (Dictations), `captions`, `bars` (the bars mark, filled), and the Mac's Record As symbols (§17.6), including `rec.braindump` and `rec.audio`. Every Control Center control uses them, so Control Center shows one family: the session switch `bars`, Record `record`, Dictate `dictate`, New thought `thought`. The Action button's control takes the name and symbol of what you chose in Settings › Action button ("Record or mark" with `mark`; `Ribbon.actionControl`), which the app mirrors into the App Group; the controls gallery, and a control the app hasn't named yet, say Verbose action.
- Icons sit bare. Home's start tiles and Record panel have no disc behind the icon. Elsewhere an icon may sit in a tinted disc of its own color; never put a white disc on a colored tile.
- **Diagrams:** Capture (rings), Refine (paths into lines), and Understand (overlapping circles). Use them in onboarding, the iPhone's Method cards, empty states, and loading. On the Record card and the tiles they become the line patterns (§13: rings, lines, circles, strands). Don't use them as wallpaper. Ink strokes on paper or on their own fill.

## 4. Marks and the app icon

- **App icon:** the notation bars (B1), dimensional: white bars with depth and a soft shadow on a persimmon-red field that lightens toward the top left (Tim's render, October 23, 2026). Masters: `brand/verbose-app-icon-render-1254.png` (source), `verbose-app-icon-1024.png` (iPhone, full bleed), `verbose-app-icon-mac-1024.png` (Mac shape). The flat paper-on-persimmon bars stay the mark everywhere else (sidebar, website, favicon).
- **Colophon:** the tangent rings (R3a), for the website, onboarding, About, and print. Never as the app icon.
- Public name: Verbose. Wordmark: lowercase 'verbose.' in the serif, persimmon period. Tagline: 'Go ahead. Be verbose.' 'Verbatim' is the internal codename.
- **In-app wordmark:** the bars plus lowercase "verbose." in the serif, as in the sidebar (`Refined.wordmark`). The period is a mark in the text-safe persimmon (`accentText`, 5.1:1 on paper); the bright `persimmon` measures 2.5:1 on paper, too low for a small mark. The tagline (`Refined.tagline`) sits under it in the serif on About and the first onboarding screen.
- **Retired:** the V wing mark (`BrandArt.mark`). It must not appear anywhere.
- One color per mark: persimmon, ink, or paper.

## 5. Surfaces and glass

1. **[checked]** Never put text on a blur or material (`.ultraThinMaterial`, `.regularMaterial`, `.glassEffect`). Glass belongs to system chrome: toolbars, tab bars, sheets, and transient controls without text. A floating bar with text uses `Refined.card` plus a hairline.
2. Paper, illustrations, and colored tiles stay opaque.
3. Radii (sharp, `Radius` in Tokens.swift): 4 for filter chips, fields, and keycaps; 6 for rows and sheets; 8 for tiles and cards; 10 for the Record panel, color panels, and big strips. Use the tokens, never a number. Don't round everything the same. Screens built on the refined kit take their radii from `RefinedRules.radius` (§13).
4. Shadows: none on cards (§13). A hairline separates a floating surface. No soft shadows everywhere.
5. Fills are opaque, with no grain. Nothing textured goes under glass.

## 7. Motion

- Use the `Motion` tokens. Ease-out or critically damped springs; no bounce in productivity controls.
- Never animate keyboard-initiated or high-frequency actions.
- Cards and tiles press in on click or touch (`RefinedPress`); they don't tilt, lift, or catch a sheen (§13). Keep hit-testing on a flat, untransformed layer.
- Home's Record meter and its hover and touch listening retired with the refined Home (§6, §13): the Record card's rings breathe instead, and the Mac's recording card shows the real level only while you record.
- The Method diagrams loop slowly (iPhone): Capture's rings swell, Refine's strands flow into clean lines, Understand's circles drift together and apart.
- Loops (the Record card's rings, the diagrams) use TimelineView with a Canvas, at most 30 frames a second, and pause when they're off screen or the app is inactive (`LoopTimeline`). Never drive a loop with state or a repeatForever animation; on macOS 26 every SwiftUI transaction is expensive.
- Screens arrive at once: no entrance stagger on navigation, so a screen never makes you wait for its content. On iPhone that means no row rise-in, no stagger, and no tab entrance.
- **Haptics** (iPhone, its keyboard, and Apple Watch; the Mac plays none). One cause, one haptic, always with something you can see. Which haptic an event plays is `HapticPolicy` (`Model/HapticPolicy.swift`, checked by `tools/test-haptics.sh` and the iPhone's self-test); the app plays through `Haptics` (`Haptics.swift`), the keyboard through `KeyboardModel`, and the watch through `WatchRecorder`. Only system feedback generators and `.sensoryFeedback`, so System Haptics and Accessibility › Touch › Vibration turn them off too. Settings › Personalization and preferences › Haptics (on by default, per device) turns every one off; it lives in the App Group so the keyboard reads it, and the watch gets it in the iPhone's context (`WatchContext.haptics`).
  - **Start:** medium impact at 0.7, played just before the microphone opens (Record, the tray, New thought, Live captions, a keyboard session). **Stop:** soft impact at 0.8 at the tap (Stop, Stop & add, Discard, Live captions' Stop or Save), not after saving; Saved and Thought added play nothing more.
  - **While recording:** pause and resume are a light tap (pause after the audio stops being written, resume before it starts again). Mark is a light tap at 0.5 while `HapticPolicy.markBuzzes` is true; Tim checks a test recording on the built-in mic and on AirPods, and if the tap can be heard, it goes to false and Mark confirms with "Marked 4:12" only. Every recording session sets `allowHapticsAndSystemSoundsDuringRecording` before it activates (`RecordingRoute.setCategory`), or iOS silences haptics while the microphone records.
  - **Results:** transcript ready (or a recording added to one) is `.success`, once per recording, only with Verbose in front and the "ready" message showing; a template that runs after it adds none. Your voice set up and Send to Reminders adding a plan's tasks ("Added 9 reminders to Studio") are the only other `.success`.
  - **Problems:** microphone not allowed, couldn't start, couldn't save, and transcription failed are `.error`. An automatic pause (a call, a route change, audio it can't save) and low storage at the start are `.warning`.
  - **Small things:** every copy, and a ribbon take Copied or Inserted, is one `.selection` tick. A note or bookmark added, a speaker suggestion used, and a move to a project are a light tap. Choices in a list, scrubber lines, and tray rows tick `.selection`. A badge change and Delete all data play none.
  - **Keyboard** (Allow Full Access only; without it nothing plays and nothing is read): dictation start and Done are the app's start and stop taps; Inserted, Copied, the format chip, and the polished swap tick `.selection`. Typing keys tap (light, 0.6) only with Settings › Keyboard › Typing haptics on, which is off by default.
  - **Apple Watch:** `.start` just before the microphone opens, `.click` on a mark, `.stop` once recording has stopped, `.failure` when it can't start.
  - **In the background** (the Action button, a widget, Control Center, Siri, Live Activity and Dynamic Island buttons) iOS plays no app haptic; Verbose doesn't try. The Live Activity shows the change instead.
- One screen is in the window at a time. A sidebar move swaps the screen instantly, with no fade. Opening or leaving a transcript slides the new screen 12 pt over 150 ms, opaque from its first frame; the old screen is gone at once. Limit Motion swaps every screen instantly. The Mac self-test counts the screens in the window after each change.
- Back comes back to the place, not the top (Mac): the toolbar's Back and Forward, View › Back and Forward (⌘[ and ⌘]), and the mouse's side buttons return to the screen as you left it, already scrolled to where you were on its first frame, with the row you opened lit as selected (`App/NavTrail.swift`; the Library's table selection). The Library comes back that way from the sidebar too; Home only by Back.
- Icons arrive fully drawn and draw on again on hover. Icon draw-ons never loop.
- Limit Motion (and the system's Reduce Motion) stills all of it: no tilt, lift, or scale, a still meter, still diagrams, icons fully drawn, and crossfades instead of slides.

## 8. Words

Google developer documentation style: second person, active voice, sentence case, serial commas, and no "simply", "just", "easily", "currently", or "soon". Name buttons with verbs ("Record", "Copy", "Transcribe again in…"). An accessibility label starts with the word the button shows ("Done", "Cancel dictation"), with any detail in the hint, so Voice Control's "Tap Done" finds it (WCAG 2.5.3, Label in Name); an icon-only button gets a label ("Done" for a checkmark). Sentence case holds everywhere: navigation titles, cards, alert buttons, and widgets ("By day", "Tab bar", "Not now", "Today's thoughts"). Voice notes are "Voice note" everywhere you read them (the stored source stays `voiceMemo`).

The on-device promise has one phrasing everywhere (Templates, Ask, You): "On this Mac with Apple Intelligence. Nothing leaves it." (or "Counted on this Mac. Nothing leaves it." where no model runs), then the clause specific to the place, in SF Pro 12.5 `dim`, never mono. A Copy that confirms says "✓ Copied" (§6.6).

Every user-visible change adds an item to the `"next"` entry of `mac/ReleaseNotes/ReleaseNotes.json` in the same commit: its platforms (`mac`, `iphone`, `watch`), one line of about 12 words (at most 90 characters), and, when it helps, a one-sentence detail (at most 200) that opens when the line is tapped or clicked. Write in this style, present tense, with no platform prefix, exclamation points, or em dashes. What's new lists each app's own platforms first and folds the rest under one "Also on" row. CI stamps that entry with the build's run number; after the build ships, a follow-up commit sets its build number (`mac/ReleaseNotes/README.md`). Both apps show the notes in What's new.

### Contrast

Every text pair the kit draws reaches 4.5:1 in light, dark, and both with Increase Contrast, and the marks that carry meaning reach 3:1 (`mac/tools/test-refined.sh`). Where the mockup's colors fell short, the kit changed them:

- A persimmon fill is #FB7350, not the mockup's #FA6B47 (ink on it measures 4.45:1); in dark it's #F27A57, because ink on the panels' deeper #E2694B measures 3.9:1.
- The Record card's hint and the Mac recorder's secondary line are full ink: ink at 80% measures 3.6:1 and #3E4A43 measures 3.4:1 on the fill.
- The ready chip lightens the fill (`chipFull`, 6.3:1) instead of darkening it (ink at 10% measures 4.1:1).
- On the Mac, an on switch in dark is #E2694B, so its white knob reads (the light accent text measures 2.3:1 there).
- The recorder's older words use `mute`, never `faint` (2.8:1). On the Mac's recording card, words still being heard are italic in the card's ink rather than lighter: any lighter ink on the fill measures under 4.5:1.
- Quiet actions on a persimmon fill (the recording card's Mark moment and Pause) sit on the ready chip's wash: the neutral wash measures 3.95:1 under Increase Contrast.
- Under Increase Contrast, cards take a `separator` edge (an off-white card on paper is about 1.1:1), and the player turns solid.

## 15. Unclear audio

## 19. Ask

Ask (mockup "Library features", section 2, October 26, 2026) answers a question from your own recordings, with numbered citations (`Model/Ask.swift`, `Model/AskRecipes.swift`, `Transcription/AskEngine.swift`). Since step 6 it's drawn on the refined kit (§13, "Chrome and the remaining screens").

1. **Find first, then answer.** Verbose ranks passages (about 70 words of consecutive lines) in the scope: BM25 over the question's words, blended with NaturalLanguage sentence embeddings where the device has one for the recording's language. The best 12 or so (at most four from one recording, about 6,000 characters) go to Apple Intelligence with the question; it must answer from them only and cite each sentence ("[2]"). Citations that don't match a passage are dropped, a sentence left with none goes, and the rest are renumbered 1, 2, 3 in order of use. Digests and action-item questions read across the scope, newest recordings first. Nothing relevant: "Nothing in Thoughts · All time matches this question, so Verbose didn't answer." Without Apple Intelligence: the matching passages and a one-line reason.
2. **The index stays on the device.** Passages and byte-quantized vectors live in Application Support's "Ask Index" (left out of backups, never synced or uploaded). A question never waits for it. Deleting a recording or moving it to Recently Deleted takes its passages out of the index at once, and answers and digests drop its citations (`AskCleanup`). 4. **Mac.** ⌘K anywhere, Edit › Find in Transcripts (⌘F, the same panel on Search since the consolidation of October 28, 2026: one search field), opens the panel over a scrim: the field, a Search and Ask switch (`RefinedSegmented`), the chips, then the result. ## 20. Apple Watch

Apple Watch capture (mockup "Library features", section 4, October 26, 2026) records a voice note or a thought on the watch and sends it to the iPhone, which transcribes it and syncs it to the Mac like any other recording (`VerbatimWatch`, `VerbatimWatchWidgets`, `Model/WatchTransfer.swift`, `Model/WatchImport.swift`, `VerbatimPhone/PhoneWatch.swift`). The watch app needs watchOS 11; the iPhone app still runs on iOS 17.

1. **Always dark.** The page is the app's dark paper, text is paper, and persimmon means record (`WatchColor` in Tokens.swift, built from the refined table; ink on the record fill). Record is the one persimmon fill at rest; New thought is outlined, as on Home (§14.10). While recording, Stop is the fill and Mark is outlined. The big buttons use `Radius.watch`.
2. **Home:** the wordmark top left, Record (an ink disc with the capture glyph in persimmon) and New thought (`iconThought`), then "Today · 6 thoughts" in dim sans, and "2 recordings waiting for your iPhone" while any wait.
3. **Recording:** "● Recording" (or "● New thought"), the time in `Refined.mono(40)` (SF Pro with tabular digits), 13 persimmon voice bars that follow your level (still under Reduce Motion and on the dimmed always-on screen), and Mark and Stop as 58 pt circles with their names under them. Double-tap your fingers to mark on a watch that supports it (`handGestureShortcut(.primaryAction)`). Record plays `.start` just before the microphone opens and Stop plays `.stop` once it has stopped; a start that fails plays `.failure` (§7). A mark plays `.click` and shows "Marked 4:12" (the time in mono) on a paper capsule for two seconds; a second mark within 3 seconds confirms the first, as on the iPhone (§18). Recording keeps going with your wrist down (the audio background mode); it stops and saves when another app takes the microphone or the watch drops under 15 MB free, and it won't start under 50 MB.
4. **Sent to iPhone** as soon as Stop queues it: a ring with a persimmon check, "Your iPhone transcribes it and adds it to the Library." (or today's Thoughts; "gets it when it's in reach" while it's away), and "Voice note · 41:05 · 3 moments" in mono. It returns to Home after six seconds or Done.
5. **Never lose a recording.** The audio (AAC, mono, 22.05 kHz) and its details are written as it records, so a recording the app was closed in the middle of goes out at the next launch. Stop moves it to the outbox and `transferFile` sends it with its `WatchTransfer` (kind, start, length, marks); Watch Connectivity queues it until the iPhone is in reach. The watch deletes the audio only when the transfer finishes without an error, and sends anything left again at launch, when the iPhone comes back, or two minutes after a failure.
6. **On the iPhone.** Watch Connectivity activates before launch finishes, so a file arrives in the background too. It's moved into the Watch inbox, then imported through the same path as a shared file (`importSources`), once per recording ID. A thought is a Thoughts recording you chose (`idea`) on the page of the day it started, its meta line saying "Apple Watch" (`ThoughtText.origin`); a voice note is a voice memo whose kind AutoLabel picks. Marks become moments from the Apple Watch (`MarkSource.watch`: "Moment 11:47 · from the Apple Watch"). An import that fails keeps the audio in the inbox's Kept folder.
7. **Complications and the Smart Stack** (WidgetKit, drawing only, from the App Group): the circular Record (the bars mark in ink on persimmon) opens the watch app recording a voice note; the rectangular "6 thoughts today · Tap to add one" opens it recording a thought; the Smart Stack card shows your last capture ("Thought · 0:42 · 9:58 AM", `privacySensitive()`) and "Tap to record", scored higher for two hours after a capture. Tinted faces draw them in one color. The count comes from the iPhone (`updateApplicationContext`, when the library changes), plus thoughts still waiting on the watch, and drops to none when today's page ends.
8. **Signing.** One switch leaves the watch app out of the iPhone app (`VERBOSE_WATCH=NO bash tools/generate-project.sh`); TestFlight retries without it if the watch app fails to sign (mac/APP_STORE.md, "Apple Watch").
